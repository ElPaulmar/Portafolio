Postmortalis - Project Breakdown

Game Designer Writer Documentation

An innovative visual novel about the value of life at the end of the world. The game combines deep narrative choices with unique autopsy and investigation mechanics.

Summary

Postmortalis was a project developed during the final year of university as our thesis project. Development was split into 2 stages: 5 months of pre-production, where we worked on the first iteration of the game design and the first version of the GDD, and 5 more months for production, where the team had to build a vertical slice.

With the vertical slice, the goal was to present the core existential narrative proposal, showing the cast range and branching player decisions, while also exploring the innovative mechanical proposal.

Team

The team had 4 people: one programmer, one 2D artist, one 3D artist, and me as the designer.

We had regular weekly meetings to share progress and solve any questions or needs from team members. As the final presentation date got closer, those meetings became more frequent.

My Role

My role focused on clearly documenting systems and mechanics for our programmer to allow fast production, while also iterating on mechanics that were showing problems, both in functionality and because of time constraints. Alongside this, I worked in all narrative-related areas, including implementing a new tool for the team.

Project Goal

The goal with this project was to convey to the player a meaningful end-of-the-world experience. The vertical slice had to be at least 15 minutes long and capable of showing all the game's key mechanics. To achieve this, we focused on the 2 core systems and on building a mystery story that was compelling but still straightforward to solve.

Design

  • Exploration Systems: This is where the player experience is delivered.
  • Autopsy Structure: This is where the player's core work happens.
  • Narrative: Since Postmortalis is a narrative game, story elements had to carry significant weight. At the same time, because the team was small, it was essential to work efficiently, which is why we introduced YarnSpinner, a tool that proved essential for project production.
